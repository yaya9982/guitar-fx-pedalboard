Setup

  1. This page must be served from a local web server, not opened directly as a file (the browser blocks microphone access on file:// pages). From this folder run: python -m http.server 8000, then open http://localhost:8000/. (Any other static server, e.g. VS Code "Live Server" or npx serve ., works too.)
  2. Plug your guitar into your PC's mic input jack.
  3. Click "Click to Enable Audio" above and allow microphone access.
  4. Pick the correct input device from the dropdown if your PC lists more than one.

Windows mic quality tips

  • Settings → System → Sound → Input → your device → Device properties → disable "Audio enhancements".
  • Control Panel → Sound → Recording tab → your mic → Properties → Enhancements tab → check "Disable all enhancements".
  • Same Properties dialog, Levels tab: make sure the input isn't sitting at 100% and clipping.
  • Windows can apply its own noise suppression/AGC at the driver level even when this page asks the browser not to; the steps above turn that off at the source.

Latency expectations

This requests the smallest audio buffer your system can run without crackling, which typically lands in the 15–35ms round-trip range through a normal mic input, good enough to play along to. A live, continuously-updating estimate shows next to the device dropdown once audio is enabled (Est. output: __ms), like a ping meter. A dedicated USB audio interface with ASIO drivers will still beat this (sub-10ms), since browsers can't access ASIO directly (that's a hardware/OS floor, not something this page can fix in software). Wired connections, closing other heavy apps/tabs, and a quiet system all help get closer to that floor. The Pitch Shifter pedal specifically adds its own ~20ms of delay by design (how delay-line pitch shifting works), independent of the numbers above.

Denoise

The Denoise toggle in the toolbar reduces steady hiss/hum, the kind you get from a weak instrument signal going into a mic-level input, especially on high-gain amp settings. It's adaptive DSP that continuously learns your specific noise profile in real time with no manual setup step (no "click to capture noise" button needed), not a literal neural network/deep-learning model, despite "AI" being the common way people describe this kind of automatic noise reduction. It adds roughly 16–17ms of latency on top of the numbers above, which is exactly why it's off by default and toggleable rather than always-on. The Strength slider trades more reduction for more tonal thinning if pushed too far; 40–60% is a reasonable starting point.

Browser

Chrome or Edge recommended (most consistent Web Audio/AudioWorklet behavior on Windows). Firefox should also work.

Privacy & Your Data

Short version: everything you play, record, and save stays on your own computer. This app has no backend, no accounts, no analytics, and no tracking. The optional YouTube player is the one exception, and it is described below.

What the app accesses

Microphone / instrument input. When you click "Click to Enable Audio", your browser asks permission to use your input device. That audio is processed entirely inside your browser tab by the Web Audio API. It is never uploaded, transmitted, or stored by this app, and closing or reloading the page discards it. Once you grant permission, the page also reads the list of audio devices on your computer, purely to fill the input and output dropdowns and to match your output device to your input device.

Screen or tab audio (optional). In the Looper, "Also capture screen/tab audio" uses your browser's own screen-share picker to record audio from a screen or tab that you choose. Your browser requires a video track to be requested alongside the audio, so you may see a screen-sharing indicator while recording. That video is never read, displayed, or saved — only the audio is used, and every shared track, video included, is released as soon as recording stops or fails to start.

Recordings. Loop recordings exist only in your browser tab's memory. They reach your disk only when you click Download, and they are lost when you reload or close the page.

Saved presets. Your presets, the autosave state, and the last YouTube link you entered are stored in your browser's local storage on your own computer, under the keys guitarfx.presets.v1, guitarfx.autosave.v1, and gfxYoutubeUrl. None of it leaves your computer. Clearing this page's site data in your browser deletes it permanently, and there is no backup.

Bundled files. The drum kit's audio files load from the same place this page is served from. No third party is involved.

The one third-party connection: YouTube

The optional YouTube player is the only feature that contacts anything beyond the server hosting this page, and it does so only after you paste a video link — nothing is contacted if you never use it. The page then loads YouTube's IFrame Player API and embeds Google's player in YouTube's privacy-enhanced ("nocookie") mode, which holds back ad-related cookies until you press play.

From that point, Google — not this app — receives your IP address and other standard request data. That connection is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and YouTube's Terms of Service, not by this page. This app receives nothing back from it other than playback controls. If you would rather avoid the connection entirely, do not paste a YouTube link; every other feature works with no network access at all.

Hosting

If you are using a copy of this page hosted online, for example on GitHub Pages, the host serves the files and, like any web host, may log ordinary request data such as IP address, browser user agent, and timestamps, and may set its own cookies, under its own privacy policy. This app neither controls nor receives any of that. Running the page from a local web server on your own computer avoids it entirely.

Your rights over your data

There is nothing to request here. This app has no backend and no database, so your audio, your recordings, and your presets never reach the authors in the first place. There is no copy of yours for anyone to look up, correct, or delete.

What is never collected

No accounts, names, email addresses, or payment details. No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, fingerprinting, or advertising. No cookies are set by this app. Your audio is never sent anywhere.
